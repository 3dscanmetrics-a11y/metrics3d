What is 3D Quality Inspection?

3D Quality Inspection, or Computer-Aided Inspection (CAI), replaces slow manual CMM measurements with high-speed full-part scanning. By capturing millions of surface data points, we can digitally overlay the manufactured physical part onto its original nominal CAD design. This instantly highlights shrinkage, warpage, and machining errors across the entire surface of the part, rather than just isolated touch-points.

Deliverables & Analytics

We provide highly visual, actionable data reports. Our metrology analytics are generated using industry-leading software like Geomagic Control X and PolyWorks.

Heat Map Deviation

Instantly visualize deviations and warpage with intuitive, color-coded 3D heat maps. Green indicates the surface is within tolerance, while red and blue indicate excess material or undercut conditions.

Tolerance Checking (GD&T)

Ensure critical dimensions, hole positions, cylinder concentricity, and surface flatness meet strict Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing (GD&T) requirements.

PDF Inspection Reports

Comprehensive, multi-page PDF reports containing 2D cross-sections, whisker diagrams, and tabular data for seamless integration into your Quality Assurance documentation.

The Technical Process: Inspection Workflow

Phase 1: Metrology Scanning

The manufactured component is digitized using high-precision structured light or laser metrology scanners, capturing millions of points across complex geometries in minutes.

Phase 2: CAD Alignment

The resulting 3D scan data (mesh) is imported into inspection software and mathematically aligned (Best Fit, Datum, or RPS alignment) over the nominal CAD model.

Phase 3: 3D Compare & GD&T

The software calculates the exact deviation of every single scanned point against the CAD surface. Specific GD&T callouts (flatness, cylindricity) are applied to critical features.

Phase 4: Automated Reporting

Customized inspection reports are automatically generated, highlighting PASS/FAIL criteria to accelerate the First Article Inspection pipeline.

Frequently Asked Questions (Technical FAQs)

How does 3D scanning compare to traditional CMM?

A traditional Coordinate Measuring Machine (CMM) measures parts by physically touching them, capturing hundreds of points slowly. 3D scanning is non-contact and captures millions of points across the entire surface in minutes, providing a complete topographic map of the part rather than just a few discrete measurements.

What accuracy can you guarantee for part inspection?

Our metrology-grade optical and laser scanners typically operate with a volumetric accuracy of up to 0.02mm, making them perfectly suited for strict aerospace and automotive tolerances.

Can you inspect parts that don't have a CAD model?

Yes. While CAD comparison is the most common workflow, we can also perform part-to-part inspection (comparing a new part against a known "golden" physical part) to ensure consistency across manufacturing batches.
