What is Scan to BIM?

Scan to BIM (Building Information Modeling) is the reverse engineering process of converting a 3D laser scan (point cloud) of a physical space into a parametric, data-rich 3D model. Unlike a "dumb" 3D mesh, a BIM model generated in software like Autodesk Revit assigns real-world properties to elements. A wall is not just a rectangle; it is defined as a wall with specific structural layers, materials, and load-bearing data. This intelligence is crucial for accurate renovations, facility management, and architectural planning.

Levels of Development (LOD) & Exact Metrics

Not every project requires millimeter-perfect rivet modeling. We tailor our modeling tolerances using the standard AIA LOD framework to ensure you only pay for the detail you need.

LOD 200: Schematic Design

Conceptual modeling. Elements are modeled as generic placeholders. Approximate quantities, size, shape, and location are provided. Best for initial space planning and high-level architectural block-outs.

LOD 300: Design Development

Accurate modeling of specific assemblies. Precise quantity, size, shape, location, and orientation. This is the industry standard for traditional architectural retrofits and engineering clash detection.

LOD 400 & 500: Fabrication & As-Built

Highly detailed fabrication/assembly level models, complete with non-geometric metadata and as-built field verification. Every pipe flange, conduit, and structural bolt is modeled to exact real-world dimensions.

The Technical Process: Scan to BIM Workflow

Phase 1: LiDAR Data Capture

Our field teams deploy terrestrial laser scanners to rapidly capture the physical geometry of the site, generating a high-density, colorized point cloud.

Phase 2: Point Cloud Indexing (ReCap)

The raw scans are stitched together and indexed into an Autodesk ReCap format (.rcp). This optimized format allows massive multi-gigabyte files to be loaded smoothly into drafting software.

Phase 3: Revit Parametric Modeling

Our BIM engineers import the point cloud into Autodesk Revit. We trace the real-world data to create parametric families (walls, doors, pipes) matching the requested LOD specifications.

Phase 4: Clash Detection (Navisworks)

For MEP projects, the new design models are federated against the existing as-built BIM in Navisworks to perform automated clash detection, ensuring new HVAC routing doesn't intersect with existing structural beams.

Phase 5: Deliverable Export

The final model is rigorously QA/QC'd against the original point cloud for deviation analysis, then delivered in native .rvt and open standard IFC formats.

Frequently Asked Questions (Technical FAQs)

What software do you use to create BIM models?

Our primary modeling environment is Autodesk Revit. We utilize advanced plugins and automated feature extraction algorithms to speed up the modeling process while maintaining strict AIA LOD compliance.

What file formats do you deliver for Scan-to-BIM?

We deliver native Autodesk Revit project files (.rvt), Navisworks federated models (.nwd), and open Industry Foundation Classes (IFC) formats for use in software like ArchiCAD or Tekla.

Can you model hidden structures?

Standard LiDAR only captures line-of-sight geometry. However, by leveraging our integrated spatial workflow, we can deploy Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR) to locate and model subsurface utilities and embed them into the final BIM deliverable.
